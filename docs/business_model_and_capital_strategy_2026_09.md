# Sports Terminal — Business Model, Capital Strategy and Long-Range Platform Plan
_Last updated: September 2026_

## Executive thesis

Sports Terminal should not compete as another consumer box-score website. The venture-scale opportunity is to become a **sports intelligence and workflow operating system**: a source-aware data layer, analytical workstation, transaction simulator, collaborative research environment, and eventually a programmable intelligence platform for professional sports organizations and serious analysts.

The NBA is the beachhead because it offers a rich combination of historical data, complex roster/contract rules, active fan and media ecosystems, and professional analytical workflows. The product should earn the right to expand only after the NBA experience is exceptionally complete.

The defensible layer is not any single public statistic. It is the combination of:

- normalized and provenance-aware sports data;
- a reusable player/team/game/season/franchise/entity graph;
- analytical workflows and saved research context;
- front-office transaction and scenario infrastructure;
- source-aware advanced metrics and formula tooling;
- collaboration, permissions, exports and reproducibility;
- eventually proprietary models and computer-vision-derived data where Sports Terminal owns or licenses the inputs and has a clear methodology.

## Why the market can support a venture-scale company

Recent sports investment research supports a broad thesis that sports is professionalizing and becoming more technology- and data-intensive.

Deloitte's 2025 Sports Investment Outlook reported continued institutional, private-equity and high-net-worth interest in sports despite a roughly 15% decline in 2024 rightsholder transaction count versus 2023. North American investors represented 55% of investment activity by volume, and Deloitte specifically noted increased attention to businesses serving the sports ecosystem, including sports technology and other near-market providers.

Deloitte's 2025 sports industry outlook argued that organizations increasingly need stronger back offices, more sophisticated talent, strategic use of data and analytics, and improved fan experiences. Its 2026 outlook describes AI as an emerging intelligence layer across sports organizations and emphasizes the financial rigor and operational professionalization that often follow external capital.

Sports Business Journal reported that the sports-tech fundraising rounds it covered in 2025 totaled at least $2.37 billion across 51 disclosed fundraising efforts, compared with $1.07 billion across the rounds it covered in 2024. That figure is not a complete market census, but it is evidence of active venture interest.

Sapphire Sport describes its strategy as investing in Seed through Series B technology companies at the intersection of technology and culture, typically with initial investments of $3 million to $10 million. Its LP network includes owners and investors across major U.S. sports leagues and global sports/media organizations. This is a useful example of the kind of sector-specialist capital and strategic distribution network that can matter to Sports Terminal.

## Initial customer segments

Use cases:
- advanced historical/current player research;
- custom rankings and comparisons;
- trade/cap exploration;
- reusable research boards;
- exports and saved views;
- Python/workspace analysis.

Monetization:
- free entry tier;
- monthly or annual Pro subscription;

## Revenue architecture

Sports Terminal should support multiple revenue streams over time without trying to launch all of them at once.

1. **Consumer subscriptions.** Pro research, saved workspaces, deeper exports, premium analytical modules.
2. **B2B seat subscriptions.** Annual per-seat or pooled-seat pricing for media, agencies and smaller professional organizations.
3. **Enterprise contracts.** Annual recurring platform contracts with organization administration, permissions, audit, support and negotiated terms.
4. **Implementation and paid enhancements.** One-time fees for onboarding, custom data mappings, bespoke reports, integrations or organization-specific workflow modules.
5. **Maintenance and support.** Recurring premium support for customers that require higher service levels or private deployment.
6. **Data licensing.** Only for data Sports Terminal owns or has explicit rights to redistribute.
7. **Future API/compute usage.** Metered access to proprietary models, data transformations or compute-heavy analysis when a genuine external developer use case exists.
8. **Perpetual/private deployment licenses.** Reserved for institutional customers that require it; pair with recurring maintenance and upgrade contracts.
9. **Marketplace economics.** Longer-term revenue share on licensed datasets, analytical modules or third-party integrations distributed through Sports Terminal.
10. **Computer-vision SaaS.** A future B2B product if Sports Terminal develops proprietary video understanding, player tracking, biomechanics, tactical recognition or automated tagging.

Advertising should not be the primary business model. It produces weaker differentiation, can harm research-product trust, and does not create the same workflow lock-in as recurring professional software.

## Product moat

A fundable version of Sports Terminal should compound defensibility through five layers:

### Data model and provenance
One canonical entity/event/time-series framework across players, teams, games, contracts, transactions, seasons and eventually other sports.

### Workflow lock-in
Users should accumulate saved views, formulas, comparisons, research boards, workspaces, alerts, scenario histories and organizational knowledge.

### Proprietary derived intelligence
Original metrics, models, entity relationships and explainable analytical outputs built on legitimately available inputs.

### Enterprise collaboration
Permissions, organization templates, audit trails, reproducibility, workflow approvals and customer-specific data.

### Computer vision and multimodal research
Eventually convert raw video and sensor-like inputs into structured events, movement features and searchable evidence. This becomes significantly more defensible than repackaging public box scores.

## Product roadmap tied to monetization

### Phase 0 — NBA product completion / design-partner stage
Goal: prove utility before optimizing monetization.

Build:
- complete static NBA data product;
- trustworthy Advanced Stats;
- full trade/cap workflow;
- research, comparison and export excellence;
- stable player/team/game entity pages;
- saved local workspaces;
- polished source/methodology layer.

Commercial work:
- interview 25–50 target users;
- recruit 5–10 design partners across analysts, creators, media or sports organizations;
- measure repeated weekly usage and time saved;
- collect specific workflows users would pay to preserve.

Do not hire a large sales organization here.

### Phase 1 — Pre-seed/seed commercial product
Goal: establish recurring revenue and a narrow ICP.

Build:
- accounts and durable cloud persistence;
- billing and entitlements;
- organization workspaces;
- collaboration and audit history;
- export/report templates;
- secure customer-specific datasets;
- production observability and support tooling.

Initial hires:
- full-stack/product engineer;
- data engineer;
- product-oriented analytics engineer;
- first design/product hire as needed;
- founder-led sales with one commercially technical generalist;
- fractional/legal support.

Key proof:
- paid pilots;
- conversion from pilot to annual contract;
- repeat usage;
- expansion in seats/workflows;
- referenceable users.

### Phase 2 — Series A-style scale
Goal: repeatable enterprise sales and stronger proprietary data.

Build:
- enterprise identity/SSO;
- more robust organization administration;
- customer data connectors;
- stronger model layer;
- optional licensed current/live data where economics justify it;
- private deployment paths;
- security certifications appropriate to customer demands;
- first computer-vision product if design partners validate the use case.

Hiring expands across:
- engineering and infrastructure;
- data science / ML;
- product and design;
- enterprise sales;
- partnerships;
- customer success/support;
- finance/accounting;
- legal/compliance;
- IT/security.

### Phase 3 — Multi-sport platform
Goal: prove that the architecture generalizes rather than rebuilding every league from scratch.

Expand into sports only when the canonical entity/event/provenance/workspace architecture can support them cleanly. Prioritize markets with:
- strong professional or enthusiast analytical demand;
- accessible or licensable data;
- recurring workflow pain;
- enterprise budgets;
- a credible distribution wedge.

## Capital strategy

Do not raise simply because sports attracts capital. Raise when money can accelerate a repeatable mechanism that already works.

A compelling pre-seed/seed narrative would be:
- unusually complete NBA wedge;
- clear pain discovered through design partners;
- evidence of recurring use;
- first paying customers or paid pilots;
- high-margin software economics;
- proprietary workflow/data assets that deepen with use;
- credible path from NBA to a much larger sports intelligence platform.

Potential investor categories:
- generalist enterprise/data/AI funds;
- sports-specialist venture funds;
- strategic sports/media investors;
- former operators/athletes/owners with useful distribution;
- infrastructure/data investors if the product evolves into a broader intelligence platform.

Select capital for customer access, recruiting and domain expertise as well as valuation.

## Long-range expansion beyond sports

The product should not jump into unrelated markets prematurely. However, the underlying architecture can deliberately generalize around:

- entities;
- events;
- relationships;
- time series;
- geospatial context;
- provenance;
- simulations;
- collaborative research;
- programmable analysis.

That foundation could eventually support:
- esports and gaming analytics;
- geospatial, weather and environmental intelligence;
- computer-vision platforms;
- scientific or engineering data exploration;
- multimodal research systems.

The strategic principle is to expand because the core intelligence/workflow architecture transfers, not because a new category sounds large.

## Explicit non-goals

- No sports betting product.
- No prediction-market product.
- No dependence on gambling monetization.
- No fabricated proprietary metrics presented as official/source-native data.
- No commercial redistribution of third-party data without appropriate rights.
- No premature multi-sport expansion that weakens NBA quality.

## Decision rule

Before building a major new feature, ask:

**Does this increase the amount of high-value research/work that a user can perform inside Sports Terminal, create reusable user/organization knowledge, improve data defensibility, or create a credible willingness-to-pay mechanism?**

If not, it is probably not a priority for the venture-scale product.
